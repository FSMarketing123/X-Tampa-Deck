X Tampa

Introduction

Franklin Street appreciates the opportunity to present to the Receiver on X Tampa, a 0.97-acre development site at 701 N Florida Avenue in Downtown Tampa with a partially completed parking podium in place.

The Receiver has identified three likely scenarios for the site. At its request, we evaluated each one with input from Franklin Street’s in-house Project Management team:

  • Finish the garage and park it, a covered land play
  • Scrape the structure and build new
  • Redesign the current configuration and build with the existing garage

For each, the pages that follow answer the Receiver’s core questions:

  • What is the cost and timeline to finish the existing garage structure?
  • What is the cost and timeline to scrape the existing structure?
  • To what extent is the existing garage functional and feasible for a new design?

Cost and timeline figures reflect preliminary estimates from Franklin Street’s in-house Project Management team.

The Site

Where the Project Stands

What any buyer inherits, regardless of which path it takes.

Structure

Partially completed post-tensioned parking podium, stopped at the fifth level. Level 5 can be finished as an open rooftop deck (275 spaces); two decks remain unbuilt (400 spaces at completion, in line with the 400-space garage in the original plans).

Foundations

Caissons installed at a cost of $5.7M. Removing them would be just as expensive, and proximity to the historic church may rule removal out.

Condition

The Partner report shows the deck as “fair, functional.” The structure has been exposed for roughly three years; any buyer will want the report updated and assigned to it.

Design & Drawings

The podium’s column grid and cores were designed for a 27–29 story tower. Rights to the drawings are held under Kast’s design-build contract; Kast has also recorded a lien on the site (page 56).

Permits & Code

Permit status must be confirmed after three years of inactivity. Any re-permitting will require design under the new Florida Building Code (page 47).

Church

The 1922 church sits on the block beside the parking structure (a separate building) and was to be preserved as an event hall. Ownership, demolition and its effect on each scenario are covered on pages 20–25.

The Historic Church

Is It a Landmark?

The 1922 church is owned by XSC Tampa Investment LLC as part of the single X Tampa parcel, with no deed restriction requiring preservation. It is not a landmark: historic status does not block demolition today, but removing the church would require new entitlements because it is approved as an event hall.

Current Designation

The church is not a City of Tampa local landmark, is not in a local historic district (Ybor City, Hyde Park, Seminole Heights, Tampa Heights), and is not on the National Register of Historic Places.

Designation Can Still Be Imposed

The Historic Preservation Commission and City Council can designate a building meeting the Sec. 27-257 criteria without the owner’s consent, and the City can act to protect it before designation (Sec. 27-260). Once designated, demolition needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Architectural Review Commission.

Downtown Precedent

In 2022 the City halted demolition of the undesignated Tarr Furniture building at 520 N Tampa St. After about four to five months and a lawsuit, demolition was allowed through a negotiated compromise, with the Tarr building’s design elements incorporated into the new tower’s façade (pages 22–25).

What Removal Would Require

A demolition permit (asbestos survey, state notice, about three weeks) and new entitlements replacing the approved event hall. A substantial change means a new rezoning or site plan approval: about four months to a City Council hearing on the City’s 2026 schedule, plus downtown design approval.

Sources: City of Tampa, 2024 Landmarks & Historic Preservation Designations; NRHP listings in Tampa; City of Tampa Ord. 2023-138; HPC minutes (Apr. 2022); FOX 13 (Sept. 2022); Tampa Code Secs. 27-181.2 and 27-184.

Lessons

  • The 2019 demolitions predate the precedent. Two older buildings on the block came down under the original permit; a filing today would face the review the Tarr building did.
  • Undesignated is not unprotected. The City acted on a building with no designation once a demolition permit was filed.
  • Removal is possible, but negotiated. The outcome was demolition with design concessions, not a clean permit.
  • Plan for time and cost. About four months to a compromise, nine months to demolition, plus litigation and redesign.
  • The church faces a higher bar. Unlike the Tarr building, it is already approved as an event hall, so removal also needs new entitlements.
Takeaway

A buyer who wants the church removed should expect a One Tampa-style process: City review, a likely compromise and months of delay. Marketing the site with the church retained avoids that risk; a buyer who wants removal can price it.

Demolition Process & Timeline

Post-tensioned slabs hold stored energy in their tendons, so demolition is an engineered, sequenced process rather than a conventional knock-down.

01

Engineered Demolition Plan

Prepared with an engineer experienced in post-tensioning, setting the sequence for releasing tendons and removing each level.

02

Shoring & Protection

Shoring is installed floor by floor; barriers protect anchorage zones, the street and the church, which sits immediately beside the garage as a separate structure.

03

Top-Down Removal

Slabs are broken out level by level from the top, releasing tendon tension as they go, until the remainder can come down at grade.

04

Clear to Caissons

Debris is removed and the site cleared down to the existing caissons, which stay in place.

Code & Permits

What the Rules Say

The rules behind each item in the matrix, and what they mean for timing.

Permit Status After Three Years

Under the Florida Building Code (Sec. 105.4.1), a permit becomes invalid if work is suspended or abandoned for six months; work is in active progress only with an approved inspection within 180 days. If a new permit is not obtained within 180 days after a permit becomes void, the building official may require removal of work performed. After three years of inactivity, assume re-permitting unless the City confirms otherwise.

Which Building Code

The 8th Edition (2023) Florida Building Code has been in effect since December 31, 2023. The 9th Edition (2026), based on the 2024 International Codes, is expected to take effect on or around December 31, 2026. Permits applied for after that date would be designed to the 9th Edition.

Standalone Parking Under CBD-1

Tampa’s CBD use table (Sec. 27-184, Table 184-A) lists off-street parking, structured or surface, as a permitted principal use in CBD-1, including special event parking. No rezoning or City Council hearing; the garage needs administrative Design District Review (pages 48–49).

Downtown Design Approval

Any change from the approved design requires downtown design approval, which applies to both a new building (Scenario 02) and a redesign on the existing podium (Scenario 03).

The Historic Church

Not a designated landmark today, but a demolition permit filing could prompt City review and designation (pages 20–25).

Sources: Florida Senate bill analysis CS/SB 902 (2019), citing FBC 105.4.1; PermitPlace, “Florida’s 2026 Building Code”; City of Tampa REZ/SU-2 schedule (2026).
